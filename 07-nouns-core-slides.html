No "a", no "the"

  • Mongolian has no words for "a" or "the". ном can be "a book" or "the book".
  • тэр means he, she and it. There is no difference.
Энэ ном.
This is a book.
Энэ хүн багш.
This person is a teacher.
Тэр нохой.
That is a dog.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 87, 256

One or many? Usually you do not say

  • English must choose: book or books. Mongolian usually leaves the word as it is.
  • ном can mean one book or many books. You know from the rest of the sentence.
  • After a number, or after олон (many), never add a plural ending.
олон ном
many books (many + book)
олон хүн
many people
Mongolian Grammar pp. 72, 76

When you do want a plural

  • The most common plural ending is -ууд (words with а, о, у) or -үүд (words with э, ө, ү).
  • After a long vowel or ай, ой it is -нууд / -нүүд: харандаа → харандаанууд (pencils).
  • For people such as family and teachers, the word нар usually goes after the noun, as a separate word.
  • Spelling to watch: хүүхэд → хүүхдүүд, сургууль → сургуулиуд.
  • Two special ones: нохой → ноход (dogs), морь → морьд (horses).
ном → номууд
books
гэр → гэрүүд
homes, gers
багш → багш нар
teachers
Mongolian Grammar pp. 73–75

Older or younger?

  • English asks: brother or sister? Mongolian asks first: older or younger than me?
  • ах = older brother. эгч = older sister.
  • дүү = anyone younger, a boy or a girl.
  • If you need to say which: эрэгтэй дүү = younger brother, эмэгтэй дүү = younger sister.
ах нар
older brothers
эгч нар
older sisters
дүү нар
younger brothers and sisters
Mongolian Grammar p. 74 (эрэгтэй дүү and эмэгтэй дүү are everyday phrases, not from the book)

Describing words go in front

  • A describing word (an adjective) always stands in front of its noun, as in English.
  • It never changes. It takes no plural and no endings.
  • олон (many) works the same way: олон ном.
том нохой
a big dog
шинэ ном
a new book
сайн найз
a good friend
Mongolian Grammar p. 213

This book is new

  • To say what something is like, put the describing word last. There is still no word for "is".
  • Word order changes the meaning: Энэ шинэ ном is "This is a new book". Энэ ном шинэ is "This book is new".
  • Add биш after the describing word to say "is not".
  • Ask with уу or үү, as in Lesson 6.
Энэ хүн өндөр.
This person is tall.
Энэ ном шинэ биш.
This book is not new.
Тэр нохой том уу?
Is that dog big?
Mongolian Grammar pp. 214, 329

Conversation

АЭнэ юу вэ?
What is this?
БЭнэ ном. Энэ шинэ ном.
This is a book. This is a new book.
АТэр хэн бэ?
Who is that?
БТэр багш. Тэр сайн багш.
That is a teacher. He (or she) is a good teacher.
АЭнэ нохой том уу?
Is this dog big?
БҮгүй, энэ нохой том биш. Энэ нохой жижиг.
No, this dog is not big. This dog is small.

ах, эгч or дүү?

You are 10. Your brother is 14. He is your ах .
You are 10. Your sister is 16. She is your эгч .
You are 10. Your sister is 6. She is your дүү .
You are 10. Your brother is 3. He is your дүү .

Put the words in the right order

шинэ / энэ / ном (This is a new book.)Энэ шинэ ном.
ном / шинэ / энэ (This book is new.)Энэ ном шинэ.
том / нохой / тэр (That dog is big.)Тэр нохой том.
багш / сайн / тэр (He is a good teacher.)Тэр сайн багш.
биш / гэр / том / энэ (This home is not big.)Энэ гэр том биш.

Your turn

  • Point at things and people in the room or in a picture and ask: Энэ юу вэ? Энэ хэн бэ?
  • Name everyone in your family with ээж, аав, ах, эгч or дүү. Remember: older or younger than YOU.
  • One person says a noun. The next person puts a describing word in front: нохой → том нохой.
  • Describe things and people in the room: Энэ ном шинэ. Аав өндөр.
  • Ask a yes/no question that should get the answer Үгүй, … биш: Энэ гэр жижиг үү?
