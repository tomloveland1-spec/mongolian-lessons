One ending for my own, your own, his own

  • English changes the word: my, your, his, her, our, their. Mongolian has one ending for all of them: -аа.
  • It ties the word back to whoever does the action. In English that usually comes out as "my own", "your own", "his own".
  • So ахдаа is "to my brother" after би, "to your brother" after чи and "to his brother" after тэр.
  • The doer never takes this ending. It goes on the other words in the sentence.
  • The book says owning is only one of its uses: the ending links a word to the doer.
  • Lessons 11 to 17 left this ending off to keep things simple. From now on, use it for your own family and things.
Би ахдаа хэлсэн.
I told my older brother.
Чи ахдаа хэлсэн үү?
Did you tell your older brother?
Тэр ахдаа ном явуулсан.
He / she sent his / her older brother a book.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 106, 108, 112

After an action: ном → номоо

  • Put the ending straight on the thing or person the action lands on.
  • Pick the vowel as always. а, у words: -аа. э, ү words: -ээ. о words: -оо. ө words: -өө.
  • After a long vowel, or after ай, ой, put г in first: цай → цайгаа, дүү → дүүгээ, нохой → нохойгоо.
  • A final ь turns into и: морь → морио, сургууль → сургуулиа.
  • With people you may keep -ыг / -ийг in front: ааваа or аавыгаа, ээжээ or ээжийгээ.
Би хоолоо идсэн.
I ate my meal.
Би цайгаа уусан.
I drank my tea.
Би ааваа санана.
I will miss my father.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 27, 110–111

After the endings you know

  • First the ending you already know, then -аа on top of it.
  • The small vowel before -д drops out: ахад → ахдаа, ээжид → ээждээ.
  • After -ын / -ийн it is -хаа / -хээ (-хоо / -хөө in о and ө words).
  • After -тай and руу it is -гаа / -гээ (-гоо / -гөө in о and ө words).

Миний … or -аа?

  • The owner is the doer: use -аа. Би ээждээ цай авчирсан. (I brought my mother tea.)
  • The owner is someone else: use миний, чиний, түүний … in front of the noun.
  • The doer itself cannot take -аа, so there you say миний: Миний аав багш.
  • ахаасаа can only be the doer's own brother.
Тэр миний ахаас ном авсан.
He / she took a book from my older brother.
Тэр ахаасаа ном авсан.
He / she took a book from his / her (own) older brother.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 106, 110, 112, 258

Little words after the noun: минь, чинь, нь

  • минь = my. чинь = your. нь = his, her, their. They are separate words and come after the noun.
  • If the noun has an ending, they come after the ending: ээжид чинь (to your mother).
  • They also work on the doer, where -аа cannot go: Ээж минь цай уусан.
  • They never point back to the doer. For that, use -аа: Би ээждээ цай авчирсан.
  • тань is the polite "your" (аав тань) and маань is "our" (аав маань).
Ээж минь цай уусан.
My mother drank tea.
Би ээжид чинь цай авчирсан.
I brought your mother tea.
Би ахад нь хэлсэн.
I told his / her older brother.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 106–107

Three ways, side by side

  • миний goes before the noun and минь goes after it. The book gives one English sentence for both.
  • -аа is different: the brother belongs to the doer, тэр.
  • The book says минь, чинь, нь show how a person and the noun are connected, more than who owns it.
  • On the doer itself only two of the three work: Миний ах … or Ах минь …

Your turn

  • Hold up your own book and say: Би номоо уншина. Then point at someone else's book: Би чиний номыг уншина.
  • Say what you have done today: Би хоолоо идсэн. Би цайгаа уусан.
  • Take turns with one sentence and change only the first word: Би ээждээ тусална. Чи ээждээ тусална. Тэр ээждээ тусална. Notice that ээждээ never changes.
  • Ask Чи хэнтэй тоглох вэ? Answer: Би найзтайгаа тоглоно. Би ахтайгаа тоглоно. Би дүүтэйгээ тоглоно.
  • Ask about someone's family: Аав чинь хаана байна вэ? Answer: Аав минь гэртээ байна. or Аав минь ажилдаа явсан.
