Seven vowels, two families

  • Mongolian has seven basic vowels: а, э, и, о, у, ө, ү.
  • Back vowels: а, о, у.
  • Front vowels: э, ө, ү.
  • и is neutral. It belongs to neither family.
а · о · у
back
э · ө · ү
front
и
neutral
Mongolian Grammar p. 20

What do "back" and "front" mean?

  • The names say where your tongue is. For а, о, у it pulls back in your mouth. For э, ө, ү it moves forward.
  • Try it: say о, then ө. Say у, then ү. Feel the tongue slide forward.
  • The book has two more names for each family. Back vowels are also called strong or masculine. Front vowels are also called weak or feminine.
  • All three names mean the same two groups, so use whichever you remember best.
а · о · у
back = strong = masculine
э · ө · ү
front = weak = feminine
Names: Mongolian Grammar pp. 7, 20

Every back vowel has a front twin

  • а pairs with э. о pairs with ө. у pairs with ү.
  • ө and ү look like о and у with a mark added. The marked letter is the front twin.
  • Swap a vowel for its twin and you get a different word.
ор / өр
bed / debt
ус / үс
water / hair
уул / үүл
mountain / cloud
Mongolian Grammar p. 20

The rule: one family in one word

  • A Mongolian word has only back vowels or only front vowels. The families do not mix.
  • This rule is called vowel harmony.
  • So one vowel tells you the family of the whole word.
  • Words borrowed from other languages do not follow the rule.
аав · олон · уул · хамар
back words
ээж · өдөр · үүл · үнэр
front words
Mongolian Grammar pp. 20–21

и goes with both families

  • и is neutral. It can sit in a back word or a front word.
  • To find the family, look at the other vowel.
  • If и is the only vowel in the word, the word counts as front.
сонин
back word with и (newspaper)
бичих · тийм · жимс
only и, so front: to write · yes · fruit
Mongolian Grammar pp. 20–21

The secondary vowels

  • Besides the seven basic vowels, Mongolian has secondary vowels: long vowels, vowels with й, the y-vowels, and ы.
  • Each one belongs to the family of the basic vowel it is built on.

ю: one letter, two sounds

  • ю is one letter, but it writes two different sounds: a Y sound (as in "yes") plus у, or a Y sound plus ү.
  • Y + у is back. Y + ү is front. That is why ю is in both columns of the table.
  • ю is not neutral like и. In any one word it is either the back sound or the front sound.
  • To know which, look at the word it is in, or the word it is attached to. Back word: say Y + у. Front word: say Y + ү.
  • Most words with ю are back words. You will meet front ю mostly in the question ending юү (Lesson 6).
юм · юу
thing · what (back: Y + у)
Энэ харандаа юу?
Is this a pencil? (харандаа is back, so Y + у)
Танд олон ном бий юү?
Do you have a lot of books? (бий is front, so Y + ү)
Mongolian Grammar pp. 20, 200, 328

Secondary vowels in words

  • A double letter is a long vowel, and it stays in its letter's family: аав is back, ээж is front.
  • ы appears only in endings, on back words. Front words use ий in the same place.
  • Lesson 3 teaches how to say all of these.
аав · хоол · сайн · яс
back: father · food · good · bone
ээж · үүл · ер
front: mother · cloud · ninety
таны / миний
your (polite) / my: ы on a back word, ий on a front word
Mongolian Grammar pp. 20–21

о and ө like company

  • When the first vowel of a word is о, the short vowels after it are said as о too.
  • When the first vowel is ө, the short vowels after it are said as ө.
  • That is why it is олон and not олан, өдөр and not өдэр.
олон · орох
many · to enter
өдөр · дөрөв
day · four
хамар · үнэр
а and э words: nose · smell
Mongolian Grammar p. 7

Why it matters: endings match the family

  • Mongolian adds endings to words, and most endings come in a back form and a front form.
  • You pick the form that matches the word. Back word, back ending. Front word, front ending.
  • You will do this in almost every lesson from now on.
ах → ахаас
from older brother (back: -аас)
ээж → ээжээс
from mother (front: -ээс)
явна / ирнэ
will go (back: -на) / will come (front: -нэ)
Mongolian Grammar pp. 20–21

How to remember

  • Father and mother: аав has the masculine vowel а. ээж has the feminine vowel э.
  • The two special Mongolian letters, ө and ү, are both front.
  • Chant the twins: а–э, о–ө, у–ү. Back first, front second.
  • и is the friend of both families.
аав
father: back, masculine
ээж
mother: front, feminine

Your turn

  • Warm-up: greet each other. Сайн байна уу? (Hello: sain bain uu). Answer: Сайн. Сайн байна уу?
  • Chant the two rows: а, о, у, then э, ө, ү, then и. Feel your tongue move forward for the second row.
  • Chant the twins: а–э, о–ө, у–ү. One person says a back vowel, the next person answers with its front twin.
  • One person says ус or үс, ор or өр, уул or үүл. Everyone else points to the one they heard.
  • One person reads a word from the word list. Everyone else points a thumb back over a shoulder for a back word, or a finger forward for a front word.
  • Say аав, then ээж. Which family is each one in?
