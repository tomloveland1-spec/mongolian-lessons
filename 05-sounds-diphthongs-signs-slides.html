A diphthong is one sound, not two

  • й is a "half и": very short. It blends into the vowel before it and makes a new sound.
  • Do not say them like English "fine" or "time", where you hear two vowels.
  • Mongolian diphthongs are held together more tightly than English ones. Keep them short.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 10, 20

The soft sign ь at the end of a word

  • ь has no sound of its own. It marks the consonant before it as soft.
  • These words used to end in и. The и shrank, but it could not vanish completely, so ь is its trace.
  • The soft consonant also pulls the vowel before it forward in the mouth.
  • The book's rough guides: хонь sounds like "hoin", морь like "myrrh".
морь
horse
хонь
sheep
сургууль
school
Mongolian Grammar pp. 12, 14, 22

ь changes the word

  • Leaving ь off gives a different word. Say each pair and listen for the vowel and the last consonant.
ам / амь
mouth / life
уул / ууль
mountain / owl
хан / хань
khan / companion
Mongolian Grammar p. 22

The hard sign ъ

  • No Mongolian word ends in ъ. It has one job only, inside "let's …" verb forms.
  • It keeps the ending -я / -ё apart from a verb stem that ends in a consonant, so the consonant stays hard.
  • Front-vowel verbs use ь + е for the same job.
  • Stems ending in a vowel need no sign.
явъя
let's go (яв-)
авъя
let's take (ав-)
ирье
let's come (ир-)
Mongolian Grammar pp. 21–22

Your turn

  • Warm-up: greet each other. Сайн байна уу? (Hello: sain bain uu). Answer: Сайн. Сайн байна уу?
  • Read down the list slowly, then fast: далай, нохой, орой, дугуй, дэлхий.
  • One person says ам or амь, уул or ууль, хан or хань. Everyone else points to the one they heard.
  • Say Сайн байна уу? ten times. Listen for сайм.
  • Say явъя, авъя, ирье. Tap the table between the stem and the ending.
