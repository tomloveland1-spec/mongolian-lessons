Four pasts side by side

  • -сан: the everyday past for anything (Lesson 9). Тэр ирсэн. (He came.)
  • -лаа / -лээ / -лоо / -лөө: it happened just now and you saw it. Тэр ирлээ.
  • -в: mostly in writing, for finished things the writer saw. Тэр ирэв.
  • -жээ / -чээ: you did not see it, it is a story, or it just happened by itself. Тэр иржээ.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 184-188

-лаа: it just happened

  • Use it for something you saw happen a short time ago. People say it often.
  • It picks its vowel like -сан does: явлаа, ирлээ, бослоо, өглөө.
  • It can also mean you are just about to do it: Би явлаа is "I'm off".
  • To ask, people normally go back to -сан: Чи явсан уу?
Би саяхан сонин ном уншлаа.
Recently, I read an interesting book.
Одоо аав ээж ирлээ.
Mom and Dad are coming now.
Би Улаанбаатар явлаа.
I'm leaving for Ulaanbaatar.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 187–188

-в: the written past

  • You will read it in books far more than you will hear it.
  • The writer saw the action, and it is finished.
  • Change the -х of the dictionary form to -в: явах → явав, ирэх → ирэв, суух → суув, асуух → асуув.
  • In speech it turns up in questions about something recent.
Би суув.
I sat down.
Туяа, чи наадам үзэв үү?
Tuya, did you see Naadam?
Mongolian Grammar p. 186

-жээ: the past you did not see

  • Use -жээ for things you did not see happen yourself.
  • Stories and things from long ago use it, like "Once upon a time".
  • It is also common for things that happen suddenly or without anyone meaning to.
  • Because you always see what you do yourself, it is not used with би or бид.
  • No vowel to choose: it is always ээ.
Эрт урьд цагт эмгэн өвгөн хоёр амьдарч байжээ.
Once upon a time there lived an old man and an old woman.
Чи худал хэлжээ.
You told a lie.
Орой болжээ.
It has become evening. (It happened without anyone doing it.)
Mongolian Grammar pp. 185-186

ж or ч?

  • Most verbs take -жээ: явжээ, иржээ, харжээ, хэлжээ, суужээ, хийжээ.
  • A small group takes -чээ. Learn these four: авах → авчээ, өгөх → өгчээ, сурах → сурчээ, гарах → гарчээ.
  • Longer verbs whose stem ends in р also take -чээ: амьдрах → амьдарчээ (lived).
  • Spelling to watch: ярих → ярьжээ, тоглох → тогложээ, туслах → тусалжээ.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 26–29

Questions and "did not"

  • Questions are not made with -жээ, and rarely with -лаа. Ask with -сан from Lesson 9.
  • "Did not" is the same for every past: -аагүй (or -сангүй) from Lesson 9.
Багш иржээ.
The teacher came. (I hear.)
Багш ирсэн үү?
Did the teacher come?
Багш ирээгүй.
The teacher did not come.
Mongolian Grammar pp. 185–187

Story

1Эрт урьд цагт нэг хүүхэд байжээ.
Once upon a time there was a child.
2Нэг өдөр хүүхэд хот явжээ.
One day the child went to town.
3Тэр талх, сүү авчээ.
He bought bread and milk.
4Орой болжээ. Хүүхэд гэртээ иржээ.
Evening came. The child came home.
5Ээж цай өгчээ. Хүүхэд цай уужээ.
Mother gave him tea. The child drank the tea.
6Тэгээд тэр унтжээ.
And then he slept.

Your turn

  • Read the story aloud, one line each. Then cover the English and say what each line means.
  • Tell the story again with a different person: Эрт урьд цагт нэг багш байжээ ...
  • One person says a dictionary form (авах). The next person says the story past (авчээ).
  • Say what somebody did while you were not there: Аав цай уужээ. Then say something you did yourself with -сан: Би цай уусан.
